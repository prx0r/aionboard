# AI Onboard: opportunities beyond UK tradespeople

> Research synthesis as of 23 September 2026. Market-size figures below were source-checked. Prices, channel tactics, and conversion expectations are hypotheses until validated through discovery calls and paid founding-client installations.

Nail technicians are a promising second market for AI Onboard, particularly independent and home-based professionals who acquire customers through Instagram and TikTok. But sell them a different installation from the one sold to electricians: direct bookings, deposits, social-media enquiries and repeat customers, rather than AI receptionists and complicated business systems.

The wider opportunity is significant, but it requires a different prospecting strategy from Companies House-based outreach.

## Checked market-size evidence

- The UK had an estimated **3.2 million sole proprietorships** at the start of 2025, within an estimated 5.7 million private-sector businesses. Source: [GOV.UK, Business population estimates 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release).
- For April 2025–March 2026, ONS Annual Population Survey estimates reported approximately **87,000 self-employed people** in SOC 6221, hairdressers and barbers, and approximately **63,200 self-employed people** in SOC 6222, beauticians and related occupations. Source: [ONS, Self-employment trends in the hairdressing and barbering sector: 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/transparencyandgovernance/freedomofinformationfoi/selfemploymenttrendsinthehairdressingandbarberingsector2026).
- These are survey-based estimates of self-employed people, not a count of active businesses. Do not treat them as a ready-made customer list.
- Many sole traders will not appear in a Companies House prospect database. A separate discovery process is therefore needed for sole traders operating primarily through social media, booking pages, and local directories.

## Competitive reality for nail-technician tooling

Do not pitch AI Onboard as “another booking app.” Booking, deposits, reminders, portfolios, and payments are already sold by established providers, including:

- Booksy for nail-salon booking, reminders, and no-show protection: [Booksy nail software](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/who-loves-us/nails).
- Nextro for upfront nail-technician booking and deposits: [Nextro nail technicians](https://nextroapp.com/nail-technicians/booking-software).
- CHAIR for branded nail-technician booking pages without marketplace commission: [CHAIR nail technicians](https://getchair.co.uk/business-types/nail-technicians).
- Glamly and Taptend for bio-link booking and deposit collection: [Glamly nail techs](https://www.glamly.uk/for/nail-techs), [Taptend](https://taptend.com/).

The defensible AI Onboard offer is therefore setup, integration, operating discipline, discovery readiness, and measurement—not a replacement booking platform.

## Discovery and compliance rules

- Social-media bios, booking links, directories, and reviews are research signals, not consent.
- Record where each contact detail came from.
- Verify contact details before storage.
- Record permission or another lawful basis before marketing contact.
- Screen proposed live marketing calls against TPS, CTPS, and the internal suppression list.
- Respect objections immediately.
- Do not automatically market to newly imported research records.

## Pilot implication

Keep electricians as the first pilot because Companies House, compliance workflows, job pipelines, and the existing installation pattern are strongest there.

Use nail technicians and lash/brow artists as the second pilot inside the existing beauty vertical. They need a narrower installation than electricians: booking, deposits, social enquiry handling, reminders, rebooking, reviews, and discovery readiness.

Do not launch separate products for all nine niches before validating unit economics, delivery time, support load, and repeatability with real paying customers.
