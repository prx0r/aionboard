# AI Onboard: UK market research and launch strategy

Research as of 23 September 2026

There's a substantial opportunity to sell AI implementation to UK trades businesses. The gap isn't simply that tradespeople haven't heard of AI. Many are already experimenting with it, but relatively few have connected it to their actual business operations.

I'd initially build AI Onboard around owner-managed electrical, heating and plumbing businesses with 2–10 people. These businesses are large enough to have recurring administration problems but often small enough that the owner still manages enquiries, quotations, staff and suppliers.

Muse could become a major part of the offer, especially given its agent capabilities and WhatsApp interface. But the service should work independently of Muse's UK rollout and existing integration restrictions.

Three commercial implications stand out.

First, AI awareness is no longer the only problem. The 18% integration figure among AI-using sole traders suggests that implementation is a separate opportunity from simply providing access to an assistant.

Second, construction businesses are relatively underserved digitally: 35% of those surveyed reported having no website. However, a missing website doesn't automatically mean a business is ready to pay for one.

Third, the survey found that 73% of businesses handling digitised data were uncomfortable with their information being used to train external AI models. Privacy, permissions and control over customer information should therefore be selling points, not afterthoughts.

These statistics establish a technology-adoption gap. They do not yet establish how many electricians would pay £499 for your particular service. That's what your first sales calls need to discover.


## 2. Muse is the opportunity to watch, but don't make it a dependency

Meta launched Muse on 8 September 2026 in the US, accessible through its dedicated app and WhatsApp. It can perform multistep tasks across authorised applications, including email, calendar, shopping and payments. Its initial release is US-only; I haven't established a general UK launch date.

Two developments make it particularly relevant to AI Onboard.

Meta has opened the Muse Connector Platform  for businesses to submit integrations, subject to functional, security and legal review. And Reuters reported on 22 September that Meta is testing human assistance for certain calls made by Muse to businesses.

That points to two potential services: helping tradespeople operate their businesses with Muse and helping those businesses receive and respond to enquiries from customers' AI agents.

But there is a distinction between a business being contactable by an AI assistant and having an official Muse connector or advertising placement. Meta's public connector submission process does not establish a general paid local-business advertising programme for Muse.

Suggested positioning

# AI Onboard — Get your trade business ready for AI.

We set up your business systems, show you how to use AI to handle everyday work, and prepare you for the next generation of AI assistants—including Muse when supported.

The strongest immediate product is the installation service. Muse-readiness can be an important selling point, but you need to deliver useful workflows now rather than sell a promised future capability.

## 7. The actual sales approach

Sell the result rather than starting with Muse's technical capabilities. An electrician who doesn't yet use AI may not care which model or connector you're configuring.

The opening question I'd test is:

> Hi, I run AI Onboard. I'm helping small electrical businesses get their email, enquiries, quotes and diary working together with AI, without needing to learn a load of new software. I'm doing a few hands-on setups at the moment. How are you currently managing your enquiries and quotes?

You can then ask what software they use, how many enquiries they receive, which administrative tasks they dislike and whether they're experimenting with AI already.

If they're interested, offer a short demonstration using fictional customer data. Show an enquiry becoming a draft reply, appointment suggestion and quotation based on an approved price list. The demonstration should make clear where a person checks and approves the result.

One important restriction: don't promise a fully automated Checkatrade integration. Its lead-delivery and booking webhooks are documented for strategic accounts, so an ordinary tradesperson may only have access to their existing app and notification emails.

## 8. A practical launch experiment

I'd conduct a tightly scoped two-week pilot before investing in development or expanding beyond electricians.

1. Build one convincing demonstration and a one-page AI Onboard website with transparent pricing and a booking link.

2. Manually research and qualify 100 electrical businesses in Greater Manchester. Screen any proposed telephone contacts before calling.

3. Make compliant calls, record objections and offer demonstrations to interested owners. Aim for five substantive discovery conversations before changing the offer.

4. Deliver up to three paid founding-client installations. Record the actual installation time, software compatibility problems, support requests and what each customer uses afterward.

5. Ask each customer for feedback, a referral if satisfied and permission to use their results in an accurately described case study.

6. Refine the standard setup package and begin charging the normal price once its delivery time and value are sufficiently understood.

Use your first calls as research as well as sales. A recurring objection such as "Tradify already does that" is valuable information. Likewise, if several contractors say they need help with their Google profile or booking process rather than Muse, adjust the offer to match.

The success measure isn't just revenue. It's whether you can deliver a repeatable installation, demonstrate a useful result and obtain referrals without ongoing unpaid support.

## 9. Where POW fits

Keep POW separate and maintain its existing data-collection work. AI Onboard should be capable of generating revenue without waiting for a robotics product, a connector approval or a marketplace launch.

There is, however, a useful bridge between them. Electrical contractors involved in industrial automation, smart homes, commercial maintenance and emerging robotics may eventually be interested in POW's specialist equipment and service intelligence.

That future relationship should be opt-in. Customer email, financial and job data collected through AI Onboard shouldn't automatically flow into POW.

The commercial opportunity to test now is straightforward: a tradesperson pays you to make their existing business systems work better with AI. If you can deliver that reliably for £499, the consultancy has a reason to exist even before Muse reaches the UK.
